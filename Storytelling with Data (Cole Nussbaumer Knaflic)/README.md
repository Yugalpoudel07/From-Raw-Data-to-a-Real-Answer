### Step 0 — Warm-up · 10 min
- [ ] 📄 [my guiding principles](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2017/8/9/my-guiding-principles): the whole method on one page (intent, the "aha" moment, eliminate the non-essential, direct attention, use words, audience first). Read it first and again at the end.

---

### Step 1 — Ch 1: The importance of context · 45 min
**Key ideas:** *exploratory* vs *explanatory* analysis · **who** is your audience, **what** do you want them to know or do, **how** will data help · the **3-minute story** · the **Big Idea** (one sentence).

- [ ] 📄 [consult for context](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2015/02/consulting-for-context): the questions to ask before you make a single chart.
- [ ] 📄 [the 3-minute story](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2014/02/the-3-minute-story): if you had 3 minutes and no slides, what would you say?
- [ ] 📄 [what's the Big Idea?](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2014/02/whats-big-idea): (1) your point of view, (2) what's at stake, (3) one complete sentence.
- [ ] 📄 [storyboarding](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2014/02/storyboarding): outline on paper or sticky notes before opening any tool.

**Do:** write the **Big Idea sentence for P1** at the top of your notebook before any charting.

---

### Step 2 — Ch 2: Choosing an effective visual · 1 h
**Key ideas:** the handful of visuals that cover most cases: simple text, tables, heatmaps, scatter, line, slopegraph, bars (vertical, horizontal, stacked, waterfall), square area · **bars must start at zero** · avoid pies/donuts, 3D, and a secondary y-axis.

- [ ] 📄 [how do I know which graph to use?](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2021/4/6/how-do-i-know-which-graph-to-use): brainstorm several options, then choose one based on your audience.
- [ ] 📄 [chart guide](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/chart-guide): the index of "what is a ___?" posts. Read these five: [line graph](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2020/3/24/what-is-a-line-graph) · [bar chart](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2020/2/19/what-is-a-bar-chart) · [scatterplot](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2020/5/27/what-is-a-scatterplot) · [slopegraph](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2020/7/27/what-is-a-slopegraph) · [table](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2020/9/24/what-is-a-table).
- [ ] 📄 [an updated post on pies](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2017/1/10/an-updated-post-on-pies): when (rarely) a pie is fine.
- [ ] 📄 [declutter a dual y-axis chart](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/declutter-a-dual-y-axis-chart): what to use instead of a dual axis. This covers the roadmap's "dishonest charts" topic.

**Do:** for your P1 finding, sketch **three** different chart types on paper and write one line on why you picked the winner.

---

### Step 3 — ⭐ Ch 3: Clutter is your enemy! · 1 h
**Key ideas:** cognitive load · **Gestalt principles** (proximity, similarity, enclosure, closure, continuity, connection) · alignment and white space · contrast · the step-by-step declutter.

- [ ] 📄 [declutter your data visualizations](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2016/3/1/declutter-your-data-visualizations): the theory (how people see, visual order, contrast, strip down then build up).
- [ ] 📄 [declutter! (and question default settings)](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2019/5/13/declutter-and-question-defaults): **a full before/after makeover**: remove border and gridlines, thicken bars, fix axis titles, label data directly, add a takeaway title.
- [ ] 📄 [let's improve this graph!](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2021/1/10/lets-improve-this-graph): a second makeover to practise on.

**Do (matplotlib translation):** `ax.spines[["top","right"]].set_visible(False)` · `ax.grid(False)` · `ax.legend().remove()` + `ax.text()` labels at line ends · left-align the title with `ax.set_title(..., loc="left")`.

---

### Step 4 — ⭐ Ch 4: Focus your audience's attention · 1 h
**Key ideas:** **preattentive attributes** (colour, size, position, bold, enclosure...) · make everything grey first, then add **one** highlight colour · the "where are your eyes drawn?" test.

- [ ] 📄 [garage sale signs and data viz: the power of preattentive attributes](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2011/09/garage-sale-signs-and-data-viz-power-of)
- [ ] 📄 [where are your eyes drawn?](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/whereareyoureyesdrawn): make the chart, look away, look back, and note where your eyes land first.
- [ ] 📄 [why to use color sparingly](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2021/1/11/the-most-important-dataviz-decision-you-make): start all grey, add colour on purpose, and use the same colour in the title text and the data it refers to.
- [ ] 📄 [use color to focus or to compete for attention](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/use-color-to-focus-or-to-compete-for-attention)

**Do:** redraw one of your 8 Week 7 charts with every series `"lightgrey"` except the one that matters.

---

### Step 5 — Ch 5: Think like a designer · 45 min
**Key ideas:** **affordances** (make it obvious how to use the chart), **accessibility**, **aesthetics**, **acceptance** · text as a design tool: action titles, direct labels, annotation.

- [ ] 📄 [Learning from design principles](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2020/6/8/delightful-redesigns): affordances, accessibility, aesthetics, iteration.
- [ ] 📄 [5 easy ways to make your data visualization more accessible](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2018/6/26/accessible-data-viz-is-better-data-viz): alt text, takeaway title, direct labels, contrast, white space.
- [ ] 📄 [annotate with text](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2015/02/annotate-with-text): every chart needs a title and axis titles; put the takeaway in the title.
- [ ] 📄 [tips for creating effective slide titles](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2020/3/5/transforming-slide-titles): write titles that state the conclusion, not just the topic.

---

### Step 6 — Apply: your P1 argument figure
Checklist from the roadmap plus SWD:
- [ ] The **title states the conclusion** (an action title, not "Sales by month")
- [ ] **One** `ax.annotate` arrow points at the evidence
- [ ] Everything grey except the one thing that matters
- [ ] Direct labels, no legend if you can avoid it
- [ ] No border, no gridlines unless they help, no decoration
- [ ] Still readable at **half size**
- [ ] Source written in small grey text at the bottom
